# Branding — Design Quest Solutions

**Purpose:** define how Design Quest Solutions (DQS) presents itself in words, so that everyone
writing for the company says the same thing.

**Status:** approved 2026-09-23. Verbal brand only.

**Built from:** [`docs/About.md`](./About.md),
[`docs/marketing/user-voice.md`](./marketing/user-voice.md),
[`docs/marketing/competitors.md`](./marketing/competitors.md).

### What this document covers

Position, pillars, voice, vocabulary, messaging by audience, and the claims you may make today.

### What it does not cover

- **Page sections, headlines and page inventory.** Those belong to `landing-page-copy.md`,
  the next step in [`docs/plans/landing-page-plan.md`](./plans/landing-page-plan.md).
- **Visual identity.** Colours, typography, logo and imagery are a separate decision.
  The theme in [`src/global.css`](../src/global.css) has not been reviewed against this brand.

## 1. Position

### The one sentence

> Design Quest Solutions builds HVAC and plumbing Revit models for design-build teams in
> Los Angeles County, and finds the code and coordination problems while there is still time
> to fix them cheaply.

### The tagline

**We find it before the city does.**

Alternate, for sections about field rework: *Caught in the model, not in the field.*

The tagline carries more edge than the rest of the writing. That is deliberate. Body copy stays
measured. See [Section 3](#3-voice).

### The core idea

Everything DQS says comes back to one thing: **problems found early cost almost nothing, and
problems found late cost a fortune.** DQS finds them early.

This is not drafting. It is engineering judgement applied while a model is being built.

### Who buys, in priority order

| Priority | Buyer | What they fear |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Design-build general contractor | They hold the design contract, so the design risk is theirs personally. A bad model becomes their RFIs, their rework, their schedule. |
| 2 | Architect or small AE principal | Redlines that keep coming back. A model they cannot hand to the next discipline. |
| 3 | MEP engineer of record | Liability. Plan check corrections. Being the bottleneck. |

### How they buy

The main path is **design-build**. The general contractor holds the design contract, hires the
MEP engineer, and DQS turns that engineer's markups into a live Revit model.

The second path is **referral**. An architect or engineer recommends DQS to whoever is paying.
In that case the reader is not the buyer, so the writing must be easy to forward.

### Where DQS works

Los Angeles County is the specialty. DQS has delivered work in surrounding counties and will
continue to.

Say it as **depth in LA County, available across Southern California**. Never draw a hard county
boundary, because that turns away Orange, Ventura and San Bernardino work DQS can do.

### What DQS does not claim to be

- Not a scan-to-BIM or as-built firm. That is the rest of the market, not DQS.
- Not an all-discipline BIM shop. No architectural, no structural, no electrical.
- Not the cheapest. DQS will lose on sticker price to offshore vendors and should let those go.
- Not a volume production house.

## 2. Pillars

Four pillars, in this order. Pillar 1 leads. The others support it.

### Pillar 1 — We find problems before submittal

**The claim:** DQS catches code, clash and constructability problems while modelling, and reports
them, rather than quietly modelling around them.

**Why it wins:** no competitor reviewed named Title 24, the California Mechanical Code, the
California Plumbing Code, or LADBS plan check. Not one. This ground is empty.

**How to say it:** name the specific code or the specific check. Vague claims about "accuracy"
sound like everyone else.

**Do not say:** that DQS guarantees permit approval, or that DQS stamps or certifies anything.
DQS is not the engineer of record.

---

### Pillar 2 — HVAC and plumbing, all day, for 25 years

**The claim:** DQS models two disciplines only, and has done so since the early days of Revit.

**Why it wins:** every competitor sells "MEP" as one bundled item inside a menu of forty
services. Nobody says *this is what we do*.

**How to say it:** name HVAC and plumbing separately and often. Treat the narrowness as the
selling point, never as a limitation to apologise for.

**Naming rule:** use **"BIM services"** to be found. Use **"HVAC and plumbing"** to be chosen.
"BIM services" is fine in navigation, search terms and casual description. It must never be the
headline, because it is the most generic phrase in this market.

---

### Pillar 3 — A model your team can actually use

**The claim:** DQS follows national CAD standards and builds models that the next person can
open, navigate and extend.

**Why it wins:** every firm claims accuracy. None defines quality. Buyers who have been burned
describe models that were technically correct and miserable to work in.

**How to say it:** be concrete. Worksets, family naming, view templates, warning counts,
hosting, browser organisation. Specifics are the proof; adjectives are not.

**Do not say:** "high quality," "meticulous," "precise" on their own. Those words carry no
information. Show what they mean instead.

---

### Pillar 4 — Real people, here

**The claim:** two owners with 25+ years each, a vetted Los Angeles team, and **an owner reviews
every model before it leaves**.

**Why it wins:** the field is anonymous. Almost no competitor names a person.

**Say this exactly.** Do not say "the owners build every model" or "the owners touch every
model." DQS works with subcontractors, and that claim would be false and easy to disprove.
The review is the promise.

**On Los Angeles:** DQS campaigns on being an LA firm, and **jurisdiction knowledge leads**.
Lead with knowing the code and knowing what LADBS rejects. Mention that the team is US-based and
lives in LA as a plain fact, not as a slogan. A competitor already owns "local, no offshoring" in
this market, so DQS wins on what it knows, not on where it sits.

**On being woman-owned:** supporting cast, never the headline. It belongs in the About section
and in credentials. It matters as a tiebreaker on public work with participation goals. Led with,
it starts a credentials comparison DQS currently loses.

## 3. Voice

### Personality: the trusted specialist

DQS sounds like the senior person in the room who has seen this before. Precise, technical,
calm, and consultative. Someone who tells you the problem plainly and then tells you what to do
about it.

DQS does not sound like a startup, an agency, or a marketing department.

| DQS is | DQS is not |
|---|---|
| Specific | Enthusiastic |
| Calm | Loud |
| Technical when it matters | Jargon-heavy by default |
| Direct about problems | Alarmist |
| Confident | Boastful |

### The competitor stance: neutral contrast

State what DQS does. Do not name offshore outsourcing as a villain.

Two reasons. Some prospects currently use offshore vendors and will feel judged. Some general
contractors outsource themselves.

Frame every comparison around **the buyer's experience**, not the vendor's location.

- ✅ "A model your team will not have to rebuild."
- ❌ "Unlike offshore firms who churn and burn."

### The depth rule: plain on the surface, technical underneath

A design-build principal and a VDC manager read the same site with different vocabularies.

| Where | How to write |
|---|---|
| Home page, hero, section intros, calls to action | Plain language. No LOD, BEP, workset or Navisworks talk. A principal must understand every sentence. |
| Service pages, process pages, code pages, FAQs | Full trade vocabulary. This is where DQS proves it is not a marketing shell. |

### Writing rules

1. **Lead with the point.** First sentence, every time.
2. **Short sentences.** Aim for twenty words or fewer.
3. **Active voice.** "We check the model," not "the model is checked."
4. **Name the specific thing.** A code, a check, a failure mode, a system.
5. **No unbacked numbers.** See [Section 6](#6-objections-and-provable-claims).
6. **Do not apologise for being small.** State the size and make it the benefit.

## 4. Vocabulary

These lists come from what buyers actually write, not from marketing language.

### Words to use — value

constructable, buildable, coordinated, clash-free, clean model, extension of your team,
single source of truth, QA/QC, field experience, local code, permit-ready, downstream,
LOD, BEP, issue list, RFI

### Words to use — the buyer's pain

rework, redlines, back and forth, cleanup, unbuildable, correction loop, full-time checker,
handholding

Use pain words to show you understand the problem. Never use them to insult another firm.

### Words to avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| cutting edge, innovative, state of the art | Appears nowhere in customer voice |
| solutions provider, leverage, synergy, seamless | Makes DQS sound like the vendors buyers distrust |
| accurate, precise, high quality (on their own) | Every competitor says these, so they carry no information |
| fast, quick turnaround, low cost | This is the offshore pitch. It is the opposite of the DQS position |
| world-class, best-in-class | Unprovable |

### How to write the company name

- **First mention:** Design Quest Solutions
- **After that:** DQS
- Never abbreviate in a headline where a reader meets the company for the first time.
- **Retire "RevitDraft."** It appears in `src/pages/index.astro` and is not the company name.

### How to name the service

| Context | Use |
|---|---|
| Headline and positioning | HVAC and plumbing Revit modelling |
| Navigation, search, casual reference | BIM services |
| Named specialties | HVAC BIM, Plumbing BIM |
| Never | "MEP BIM" as the only description; it buries the specialty |

## 5. Messaging by audience

Same position, different entry point.

### Design-build general contractor — priority 1

**Their fear:** they hold the design contract, so the design risk is theirs.

**Lead with:** problems found before submittal and before the field.

**Talk about:** RFI volume, plan check corrections, schedule slip, coordination that happens
while the design develops rather than after it, a model the trades will not have to rebuild.

**Proof they want:** the QA process, a real catch, someone who picks up the phone.

**Their objection:** "Can two owners handle our volume?" See [Section 6](#6-objections-and-provable-claims).

### Architect or small AE principal — priority 2

**Their fear:** endless redlines, and a model they cannot hand onward.

**Lead with:** capacity without payroll, and a model the next discipline can use.

**Talk about:** working inside their templates and standards, the same people every time,
same time zone, questions answered the same day.

**Proof they want:** to see a model and its browser organisation.

### MEP engineer of record — priority 3

**Their fear:** liability and plan check corrections.

**Lead with:** DQS reads markups properly and asks questions instead of guessing.

**Talk about:** the issue list, code checks, real fittings, hanger and service clearances,
equipment connections, maintenance access.

**Boundary:** DQS models. The engineer stamps. Never blur this.

### The referral reader

Sometimes the reader is recommending DQS, not hiring it. Write so a page can be forwarded with
one line of introduction and still make sense.

## 6. Objections and provable claims

### The four objections

| Objection | What they are really asking | The answer |
|---|---|---|
| "You cost more than an offshore team." | Will I get that money back? | Compare total cost, never hourly rate. A cheap model that needs senior staff hours to fix costs more than a clean one. Never discount to match. |
| "Can you handle our volume?" | Is this firm too small? | Be exact: DQS runs up to five projects at once with a vetted LA team, and an owner reviews every model before delivery. Quality does not vary by who got assigned. |
| "How do I know the quality is real?" | Prove it before I risk a project. | Offer a small paid pilot or a review of a model they already have. |
| "We already have a vendor." | What would make me switch? | Ask one question: how many hours did your team spend fixing the last model you received? |

### What DQS can say today

These are true now and need nothing gathered first.

- Two owners, 25+ years each in HVAC and plumbing engineering.
- Early Revit adopters.
- HVAC and plumbing only, commercial buildings.
- Woman-owned.
- Los Angeles County specialty, work delivered in surrounding counties.
- All production staff are US-based and live in Los Angeles.
- Up to five concurrent projects.
- An owner reviews every model before delivery.
- Work follows national CAD standards.

### What DQS must not say yet

Nothing on this list has evidence behind it today. Do not publish any of it until it does.

- Any project count, square footage, or years-in-business figure that nobody has verified.
- Any named building or named client.
- Any testimonial or quote.
- Any claim about money or time saved, including industry rework statistics.
- Any turnaround or response-time promise stated as a number.
- Any certification — WBENC, California SB, or an LA agency programme — until it is held.
- "Our QA process" as a noun, until it is written down.

### Proof to gather

Roughly in order of cost. The first two are nearly free and would strengthen the brand most.

1. **Write the QA checklist.** Buyers ask for one and never get it. This is what makes Pillar 4
   real rather than rhetorical.
2. **Prepare a sanitised sample model** a prospect can open. For a craftsmanship brand, letting
   someone open the model is the strongest proof that exists.
3. **Interview three past clients.** One question: what did the last modelling vendor get wrong?
4. **Write up two or three catches** — a real code or clash problem found before submittal.
5. **Decide a response-time commitment** and publish it as a number.
6. **Start certification paperwork** if public-sector work is wanted. It takes months.
