# Icon West, Inc.

- **Website:** https://icon-west.com
- **BIM page:** https://icon-west.com/bim-modeling-services-in-los-angeles/
- **Location:** 11620 Wilshire Blvd #440, Los Angeles, CA 90025
- **Reviewed:** 2026-09-23

## What they actually are

**Icon West is a general contractor and design-build firm, not a BIM consultancy.** They appear
in "BIM companies in Los Angeles" listicles because they publish a page about their in-house BIM
capability. That makes them a *substitute*, not a direct competitor — but a substitute worth
understanding, because they represent the "we do it ourselves" option.

## How they describe their BIM work

- An in-house **BIM Manager** coordinates all parties and delivers LOD 400 and LOD 500.
- They build a virtual version of the building from owner-provided files to guide construction.
- **Subcontractors prepare the 3D shop drawings** in Revit, CAD or Tekla depending on trade.
  Icon West integrates them in Navisworks to find conflicts.
- Focus on MEP, structural and underground utility clashes.
- They host periodic BIM coordination meetings and keep the model current with RFIs.
- At closeout they deliver LOD 500 as-built conditions with asset data for facility maintenance.
- Tools named: Navisworks, AutoCAD, Revit, Bluebeam.

## Positioning

- BIM is framed as a **risk and cost control tool** for the owner, not a product: *"error
  detection, conflict resolution, and cost mitigation."*
- Credibility comes from construction delivery and client relationships. A UCLA Director of
  Physical Plant and Project Management is quoted on the BIM page.
- Sustainability and LEED are prominent elsewhere on the site.

## Strengths

- Real LA projects and real institutional clients, including UCLA.
- Owns the client relationship end to end. If a GC like this handles BIM internally, an outside
  modelling firm never gets invited.

## Weaknesses and gaps

- **They do not model MEP themselves.** They explicitly push shop drawings down to
  subcontractors and only *integrate* the results. That is a coordination role, not a modelling
  role.
- **Their BIM starts at construction.** Nothing here helps an architect or engineer before
  permit submittal.
- **Quality depends on whoever the subs hired.** They have no control over how well the
  mechanical or plumbing model is built.

## What DQS can take from this

Icon West shows where DQS fits in a GC's workflow rather than against it:

- GCs like this need someone to produce or clean up trade models before Navisworks integration.
  If the sub's model is sloppy, coordination meetings become expensive. DQS can sell directly
  into that pain.
- The "before submittal" window is wide open. Icon West's BIM story begins after design is done.
  DQS catches errors *before* plans go to the city, which is a different and earlier sale.
- Institutional LA clients (universities, public agencies) value documentation quality and
  standards compliance. That is DQS's strongest suit, and a woman-owned certification can matter
  for these buyers' participation goals.
