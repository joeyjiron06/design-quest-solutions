# Competitor Landscape — BIM / Revit MEP Services in Los Angeles County

**Prepared for:** Design Quest Solutions (DQS)
**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Scope:** Firms that show up when an LA-area architect or general contractor searches for BIM,
Revit, or MEP modelling services.

---

## 1. How this research was done, and what it does not cover

Landing pages for each firm were read directly and summarised in
[`docs/marketing/competitors/`](./competitors/). Everything below comes from what these
companies say about themselves in public.

**Please read these limitations before acting on this document:**

- **Marketing copy is not evidence.** Claims about quality, expertise and local presence were
  recorded, not verified.
- **Three firms could not be read.** Kelar Pacific, Technostruct and several others block
  automated access. Kelar Pacific in particular (a Southern California Autodesk partner and BIM
  consultancy) is likely a real competitor and deserves a manual look.
- **A widely cited listicle is unreliable.** An article titled "Top 6 Outstanding BIM Companies
  in Los Angeles" lists **JES** with a Grand Avenue, DTLA address. Reading jesbim.com shows a
  **Dubai/UAE firm** — their project list is Zayed National Museum, Dubai Metro Route 2020 and
  DWC Airport. The LA address appears to be fabricated. Treat that article, and others like it,
  as SEO content rather than market intelligence.
- **The biggest blind spot is offline.** DQS's real competition may be firms that win work
  through relationships and never rank in search results: small local drafting shops, MEP
  engineering firms with in-house modellers, and the modellers already on staff at architecture
  firms. Search cannot find those. Ask current and prospective clients who else they considered.

## 3. What they all say (and why it stops working)

Read ten of these sites and the same eight claims appear:

1. **"Accurate and precise."** Universal. Nobody advertises sloppy models, so the word carries
   no information.
2. **A menu, not a specialty.** Architectural + structural + MEP + scan-to-BIM + clash detection
   + 4D + 5D + rendering + Revit families. Everyone offers everything.
3. **Scan-to-BIM is the hero service.** Reality capture and LiDAR dominate. The market has
   drifted toward documenting buildings that already exist.
4. **Clash detection as the payoff.** "Catch conflicts before construction" appears everywhere,
   almost always framed for the construction phase.
5. **Level-of-Development language.** LOD 100 through 500 used as a proxy for rigour.
6. **Tool name-dropping.** Revit, Navisworks, AutoCAD, Bluebeam, Revizto.
7. **Geo pages without geography.** "BIM Services in Los Angeles" pages that contain no LA
   address, no LA staff, no LA projects and no California code reference.
8. **Efficiency and cost savings.** Reduce rework, reduce risk, save money.

**The consequence:** these sites are interchangeable. A buyer comparing three of them has almost
nothing to decide on except price and response time. That is a weak field, and a specific,
credible, specialised message will cut through it easily.

## 4. What almost nobody says — the gaps

These are the openings. Each one was checked across every firm reviewed.

### Gap 1 — Design-side modelling from engineer markups

**Who fills it today: essentially nobody.**

The market is built around **capturing existing conditions** (scan-to-BIM, as-builts) and
**construction-phase coordination** (integrating trade shop drawings). DQS's actual service —
taking an engineer's PDF markups and building a live, coordinated Revit model before the project
goes to the city — is barely described by anyone.

This is the single clearest gap, and it happens to be DQS's core business.

### Gap 2 — California and LA code knowledge

**Who fills it today: nobody credibly.**

Not one firm named Title 24, the California Mechanical Code, the California Plumbing Code,
LADBS, or any LA County plan-check process. BIM Pro US claims *"expertise in local standards"*
but names no standard — the same sentence appears on their pages for twenty other states.

A modelling firm that catches code problems **before submittal** is providing engineering
judgement, not drafting. Nobody is selling that.

### Gap 3 — HVAC and plumbing as a specialty

**Who fills it today: nobody.**

"MEP" is always sold as one bundled acronym. United-BIM comes closest, offering separate
"Mechanical/HVAC Modeling" and "Plumbing Modeling" pages — which proves the search demand
exists — but it is still a menu item in a catalogue of forty services.

No firm in LA County says: *this is what we do, all day, for twenty-five years.*

### Gap 4 — Model usability and CAD standards

**Who fills it today: nobody.**

Every firm claims accuracy. None defines quality. Nobody mentions:

- National CAD Standard compliance
- Model organisation, worksets, naming conventions, view templates
- Whether the client's own team can actually open, navigate and extend the model
- What happens to the model after handover

For clients who have been burned by a cheap model that was technically correct and miserable to
work in, this is a real and unaddressed pain.

### Gap 5 — Named human experts

**Who fills it today: only Dimensions.**

Dimensions puts its CEO on the homepage with a quote. Almost everyone else is faceless. No
owners, no bios, no years of experience, no photographs.

DQS has two owners with 25+ years each who were early Revit adopters. In a field of anonymous
service catalogues, that is a significant and easily communicated asset.

### Gap 6 — Serving architects rather than contractors

**Who fills it today: partially.**

Most firms optimise for general contractors and trade contractors — shop drawings, prefab
spooling, field coordination. Architects are usually listed but rarely addressed with their own
problems and language.

DQS's stated clients are architects and general contractors. The architect half of that is less
contested.

### Gap 7 — Verified woman-owned certification in LA

**Who fills it today: Tejjy, nationally — but not locally.**

Tejjy is 8(a) / WOSB / MBE / DBE / WBE certified and on the GSA schedule. They run federal
campaigns aimed at GSA, USACE and VA. **No LA-local firm reviewed makes this claim.**

LA County is full of agencies with small-business and women-owned participation goals: LA Metro,
LAUSD, LADWP, City of LA, LA County, Port of LA, LAWA. A **certified, LA-local, woman-owned**
MEP BIM specialist has a combination that nobody else in this set can offer.

## 5. Where DQS should compete — a recommended position

Combine the gaps into one sentence:

> **Design Quest Solutions is Los Angeles County's HVAC and plumbing BIM specialist. We turn
> your engineers' markups into meticulously built Revit models that follow national CAD
> standards, and we catch code and clash issues before your plans reach the city.**

Four pillars support it:

| Pillar | The claim | Why it beats the field |
|---|---|---|
| **Specialisation** | HVAC and plumbing only, for commercial buildings | Everyone else sells "MEP" inside a 40-item menu |
| **Local code fluency** | LA County and California code; we catch errors pre-submittal | Nobody names a single code |
| **Craftsmanship** | National CAD standards; models your team can actually use | Everyone says "accurate"; nobody defines quality |
| **Real people** | Two owners, 25+ years each, early Revit adopters, US-based | The field is anonymous; TITAN AEC proves "no offshoring" sells here |

**Woman-owned belongs in the supporting cast, not the headline.** Tejjy has run that play
nationally for years with stronger credentials. Used as a tiebreaker for public and
participation-goal work, it is valuable. Used as the lead message, it invites a comparison DQS
loses on paperwork.

## 6. Practical moves, in priority order

### Immediate — website and messaging

1. **Write separate pages for HVAC BIM and plumbing BIM.** United-BIM and BIM Pro US both split
   these out, which means people search for them. DQS can rank for these terms with far more
   substance behind the words.
2. **Write the code page nobody else has.** Title 24, California Mechanical Code, California
   Plumbing Code, LADBS plan check, common rejection reasons. This is defensible, genuinely
   useful, and impossible for an offshore vendor to copy convincingly.
3. **Put the owners on the homepage.** Names, photographs, years, the Revit early-adopter story.
   Copy what Dimensions does; almost nobody else does it.
4. **Publish sample models.** As-Built LA offers downloadable AutoCAD, Revit and SketchUp
   samples. For a firm whose differentiator is craftsmanship, letting a prospect *open the model*
   is the strongest possible proof.
5. **Define quality explicitly.** Publish the standard — worksets, naming, view templates, what
   you check before delivery. Turn a vague adjective into a checklist.
6. **Build two audience pages: For Architects and For General Contractors.** MAX 360's
   segmented navigation is the one good idea on their site.

### Short term — proof and credibility

7. **Get certified.** WBENC WBE, California SB, and LA-area agency SBE programmes (Metro, City
   of LA, LAUSD). A certificate number a public agency can verify is worth far more than the
   phrase "woman-owned" on a homepage.
8. **Prepare the price objection answer.** DQS will lose on sticker price to offshore vendors.
   Have a written, specific answer about rework, RFIs, failed plan checks and coordination hours
   — ideally with a real example.
9. **Publish two or three LA case studies.** Ideally ones where a pre-submittal catch saved the
   client money or schedule. That story is DQS's unique proof and nobody else can tell it.

### Ongoing — relationships

10. **Approach Dimensions and As-Built LA as referral partners, not rivals.** They capture
    existing conditions and stop. Their clients still need new HVAC and plumbing designed into
    those models.
11. **Target GCs that coordinate but do not model.** Icon West explicitly pushes MEP shop
    drawings down to subcontractors and only integrates the results in Navisworks. When a sub's
    model is poor, coordination gets expensive. That is a direct sales opening.

## 7. Risks and things to watch

- **TITAN AEC is the closest real competitor on positioning.** They already own "local,
  US-based, no offshoring" in LA. DQS should not try to out-shout them on that alone — the win
  comes from adding discipline depth and code fluency, which they lack. Note their site footer
  reads "© 2022" and events stop at 2022; **verify whether they are still actively selling
  before treating them as the primary threat.**
- **Price pressure is structural and permanent.** Offshore vendors will always undercut DQS.
  Specialisation and code knowledge are the defence, but some clients will simply buy on price.
  Accept those losses rather than discounting into them.
- **Certification takes months.** If public-sector work is part of the plan, start the paperwork
  now, not when a bid appears.
- **Claims must be true.** Several competitors are exposed here for hollow local claims. DQS
  should be specific enough to be checkable — and then be checkable.
- **Unverified competitors remain.** Kelar Pacific, Technostruct, and any relationship-driven
  local drafting shops were not assessed. Follow up manually.
